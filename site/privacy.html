Privacy Policy

Effective 12 September 2026 · Version 1.0.0

Minimal Compass has no accounts, no sign-in, and no advertising. It never uploads your heading, the position your phone computes, or anything your camera sees. What it does send is a small, anonymous set of usage counters and crash reports, and this page explains exactly what those contain.

The short version. Sensor readings, the location fix used for true north, and camera frames are processed on your phone and are never transmitted or stored. Anonymous usage counts and crash diagnostics are sent to Google Firebase, which attaches a random app-instance ID and derives an approximate city-level location from your IP address. No advertising identifier is collected, no profile is built, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

What stays on your device

Compass and level readings

The heading and the bubble level come from your phone's accelerometer and magnetometer. Android grants access to these sensors without asking for a permission. The readings are used to draw the screen and are discarded immediately. They are never written to storage and never leave the device.

The location fix used for true north

Magnetic north and true north differ by an angle called magnetic declination, which depends on where you are standing. True north is on by default, so if you grant the location permission the app asks Google Play services for a single coarse position fix once per launch, uses it to compute that one correction angle, and keeps only the angle. Decline the permission, or switch the reading back to MAG, and no fix is requested.

  • The permission requested is ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, not the precise variant. Declination changes by roughly a degree over tens of kilometres, so a city-block fix is as good as a precise one here.
  • Your coordinates exist only in memory, for as long as the calculation takes. They are never stored, logged, or transmitted anywhere.
  • The app works fully without this permission. Refusing it leaves the compass on magnetic north, which is still correct.
  • The fix itself is obtained by Google Play services, which is part of the Android system rather than this app, and is governed by Google's own privacy policy.

Camera

The AR overlay draws cardinal directions over the live camera view. The camera runs only while that view is open, and closing it releases the camera immediately.

  • Frames are rendered to the screen and thrown away. Nothing is recorded, photographed, saved, or uploaded.
  • No image analysis of any kind runs on the frames.
  • Declining the camera permission disables only the AR overlay. Every other part of the app is unaffected.

What is sent off the device

The app uses two Google Firebase services. Google acts as a data processor for both; their handling is covered by the Firebase privacy documentation.

Firebase Analytics — seven anonymous counters

These exist to answer one question: which parts of the app earn their place. They are deliberately coarse. There are no button taps, no funnels, no session replay, and no name, email address or account of any kind. Automatic screen-view logging is switched off. The events this app sends itself are:

EventWhat it records
compass_session How many seconds the app was in the foreground, and how many seconds passed before the heading settled.
accuracy_state One word describing magnetometer confidence for the session — for example HIGH or UNRELIABLE.
ar_opened That the AR view was opened, and whether it opened automatically or by a tap.
level_opened That the bubble level page was viewed.
daylight_toggled That high-contrast daylight mode was switched on or off.
location_outcome Whether the location permission was granted or denied.
declination_outcome Whether a true-north correction was successfully obtained.

Every value above is a number or a fixed word. None of them contains a heading, a coordinate, a place name, a timestamp of where you were, or anything that describes you.

Two things Firebase adds that the table above does not show. Both are disclosed on this app's Data safety listing on Google Play, and both come from Firebase rather than from anything this app chooses to send.

  • An app-instance ID. Firebase Analytics assigns each installation a random identifier and attaches it to the counters above. It is not your name, your account or your advertising ID, and it is not shared with anyone. Reinstalling the app produces a new one, though counts already recorded under the old identifier are not thereby removed. Google Play classifies it as a device identifier, and this app declares it as one.
  • Coarse location derived from your IP address. Every network request carries an IP address, and Google derives an approximate location from a masked version of it on their servers — typically a city or region. This is separate from, and much less precise than, the location fix described above: that one is computed on your phone and never leaves it. The app neither sees nor stores the IP-derived location.

The advertising identifier is removed. Firebase normally collects Android's advertising ID and sets ad-personalisation signals. Both are switched off in this app, and the advertising ID permission is stripped out of the app package entirely, so it cannot be collected even in principle. Minimal Compass contains no advertising and no advertising SDKs.

Firebase Crashlytics — crash diagnostics

When the app crashes or detects that the sensors have gone silent, a diagnostic report is sent so the fault can be found and fixed. A report contains, among other standard diagnostics, the stack trace, your device model, its Android version and free memory, the app version, a Crashlytics installation identifier that ties reports from the same install together, and one extra field recording whether the magnetometer was healthy at the moment of failure. It contains no personal data, no location and no camera data.

What the app never does

  • No account, sign-in, email address, name or phone number.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no ad networks, no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No contacts, photos, files, microphone or call data.
  • No selling or renting of data. No sharing with any third party other than Google as the processor of the two services named above.
  • No analytics profile that could be traced back to you.

Your choices

  • Permissions. Location and camera can be revoked at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Minimal Compass → Permissions. The compass keeps working.
  • Analytics and crash reports. These have no in-app switch in this version. Uninstalling the app stops all collection. If you would prefer an in-app opt-out, say so by email — it is a reasonable request and will be considered for a future release.
  • Access and deletion. Because nothing collected identifies you, there is no record that can be located and returned or erased on request. If you believe otherwise in your particular case, write to the address above and it will be investigated.

Retention

Analytics events are retained by Google for the period set on the Firebase project and are then deleted automatically. Crash reports are retained for Firebase's standard crash-reporting period. Neither is exported anywhere else or combined with any other source.

Children

Minimal Compass is suitable for users of all ages and contains no user accounts, no user-generated content, no purchases, no advertising, and no social features. It is not directed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included.

International transfers

Google processes Firebase data on servers that may be located outside your country, including in the United States, under Google's own safeguards for international transfers.
